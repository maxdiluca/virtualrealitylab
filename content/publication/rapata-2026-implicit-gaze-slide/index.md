abstract: Current commercial Mixed Reality (MR) headsets allow users to run applications in a variety of use contexts.
  When text entry is required, common MR techniques rely on visible hand gestures in mid-air to select keys on a
  virtual keyboard that can cause fatigue and draw attention from bystanders. Even techniques that use eye-gaze
  to minimize hand motions depend on visible hand gestures in mid-air for selection, as well as adding unintentional
  effort when users try to control their gaze direction. We present a novel interaction technique, Implicit Gaze+Slide
  aimed at easy and private text entry. First, it measures eye gaze without rendering the explicit gaze location
  to the user, enabling a fast and low-effort selection of a keyboard area. Then a one-handed finger slide over
  a phone surface is used for fine selection. In contrast to previous works, the hand interaction is not viewed
  by the user or the glasses, and can be hidden in a pocket from standers. In a controlled user study comparing
  Implicit Gaze+Slide with an explicit-gaze baseline, Implicit Gaze+Slide achieved a comparable entry rate (11.51
  wpm), a lower raw character error rate (5.49%), and lower perceived physical and mental demand. In a separate
  privacy-perception survey, Implicit Gaze+Slide was rated as substantially more discreet than representative mid-air
  alternatives.
